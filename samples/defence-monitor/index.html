Defence Portfolio Monitor

How I would track a defence-focused fund's holdings · Illustrative fund with five companies

Prepared 21 Nov 2026

Defence companies don't behave like the rest of a portfolio. Revenue arrives in large, late contracts from a handful of buyers, so the usual quarterly P&L review spots problems too late. This monitor tracks the leading signals instead: order book, procurement pipeline, receivables and valuation, every month.

What makes defence monitoring different

Long procurement cycleFrom Acceptance of Necessity to contract can take years. Track each opportunity by stage, not just the order book.
Few buyersMinistry of Defence, DPSUs and a few primes. Customer concentration and payment delays are the main risks.
Policy as a driverIndigenisation lists, DAP categories and export authorisations can add or remove whole markets.
Hard to valueUnlisted stakes need a regular cross-check against listed defence companies to keep marks credible with investors.

How I would set this up in the first 30 days

Week 1Agree one monthly data request with each company: order book, pipeline by stage, receivables, cash and covenants.
Week 2Build the order book and pipeline tracker, plus alerts for procurement news that touches portfolio companies.
Week 3Set up the valuation cross-check with the listed peer set, so quarterly marks come with a documented range.
Week 4Produce the first monthly monitor, and a quarterly investor pack drawn from the same data.
How this page is produced Companies send one standard monthly file. A Python script combines it with listed-peer data and procurement announcements, applies the fund's limits, and writes this page and the investor pack. Once set up, each month's monitor takes minutes, not days.
