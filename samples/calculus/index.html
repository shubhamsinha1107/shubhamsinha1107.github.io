Monitoring the Texmaco Defence investment

A one-page plan for Calculus Defence Fund's stake in Texmaco Defence Technologies (TDTL)
Prepared 21 Nov 2026
Public sources only

Calculus has agreed to invest up to ₹200 Cr for 30% of TDTL, the defence subsidiary of listed Texmaco Rail & Engineering, in two tranches. A minority stake in a subsidiary of a listed group, paid in tranches, carries risks a standard portfolio review won't catch. This is how I would monitor it.

What makes this stake different

The parent is also the main partnerTDTL can share orders, people, plant and suppliers with Texmaco Rail. Value can move between the two through pricing and order allocation.Watch: dealings with the parent as a share of TDTL's revenue and costs, and how new defence orders are booked between the two.
The second tranche is still to comeThe second tranche is the fund's main lever. Each rupee should go in only once the conditions are met and tranche 1 has been used as planned.Watch: use of tranche 1 funds against the plan, and every tranche 2 condition, with its status and date.
The listed parent is a free data feedTexmaco Rail must disclose orders, results and material events to the stock exchanges. Those filings often show what's happening at TDTL before the monthly report arrives.Watch: a daily alert on parent filings, plus defence-segment figures in its quarterly results.
A 30% stake depends on its rightsWithout control, the shareholder agreement is the protection: board seat, veto rights, information rights, anti-dilution and exit terms.Watch: a checklist of reserved matters, so no decision that needs the fund's consent slips through.

Questions I would ask in week one

  1. What exactly unlocks tranche 2, and by when?Milestones, conditions and long-stop dates in the share subscription agreement.
  2. How are defence orders split between Texmaco Rail and TDTL?Is there a non-compete or an order-allocation agreement, and who decides on new bids?
  3. What does TDTL buy from or sell to the parent, and how is it priced?Plant, people, raw material and shared services.
  4. Which decisions need the fund's consent?The reserved-matters list, board composition and information rights.
  5. What exit route did both sides agree on?IPO timeline, put or call options, or right of first offer to the parent.
